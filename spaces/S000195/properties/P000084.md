If $\alpha \geq \omega+\omega$, then any neighbourhood of $\alpha$ contains point $\omega+n$ for some $n\in\omega$, and any two neighbourhoods of $\alpha$ and $\omega+n$ contain a cofinite subset of $\omega$, and so intersect. It follows that $X$ is not locally Hausdorff at any point $\alpha \geq \omega+\omega$.
