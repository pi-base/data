Take some $c\in E$.
The projection map $(x,y)\mapsto y$ maps $L(c)$ homeomorphically onto $[0,\frac12]$
and maps $X\cap L(c)$, which is a closed subset of $X$, homeomorphically onto $Y:=[0,\frac12]\cap\mathbb Q$.

Now $Y$ contains a closed set homeomorphic to {S27}.
For example, choose irrational numbers $\alpha,\beta$ with $0<\alpha<\beta<\frac12$.
The set $Z:=(\alpha,\beta)\cap\mathbb Q$ is closed in $Y$.
Since $Z$ is countable, order-dense and without maximum or minimum element,
it is order-isomorphic to $\mathbb Q$ by
[Cantor's isomorphism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor's_isomorphism_theorem).
And since $Z$ is dense in $(\alpha,\beta)$, its order topology coincides with its induced subspace topology.
So an order-isomorphism as above is a homeomorphism between $Z$ and $\mathbb Q$.

It follows that $X$ contains a closed set homeomorphic to {S27}.
Since {S27|P55}, neither is $X$.
