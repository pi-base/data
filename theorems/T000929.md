*Notation*:  Write $x\le y$ when $x\in\overline{\{y\}}$ (specialization order).
The smallest open neighborhoods of a point $a\in X$ and of a set $A\subseteq X$ are the upper sets
${\uparrow}a:=\{x\in X:a\le x\}$ and ${\uparrow}A:=\bigcup_{a\in A}{\uparrow}a$.
And let ${\downarrow}a:=\{x\in X:x\le a\}$. By {T174} the space is {P1}, which makes this a partial order.

We say that a subset $D \subseteq X$ is a directed lower subset if it is lower, i.e. $D=\downarrow D$, and it is directed:  $\forall x,y \in D$ there exists $z \in D$ s.t. $x\leq z$ and $y\leq z$.

If $F$ is a directed lower set then it is closed since it is a complement of an upper set. Now pick two open sets $U,V$ that meet $F$, and pick $x\in U \cap F$ and $y \in V \cap F$. By directedness there is $z\in F$ such that $x\leq z$ and $y \leq z$, and since $U,V$ are open they are up-sets, hence $z \in U \cap V \cap F$. So there are no disjoint open sets in $F$, which makes $F$ irreducible (= {P39}).

Conversely, if $F$ is irreducible closed, then it is a nonempty down-set. For directedness, take any $x,y\in F$, so sets $\uparrow x$ and $\uparrow y$ are both open and meet $F$ at $x,y$ respectively. This gives $z \in \uparrow x \cap \uparrow y \cap F$, which means that $x \leq z$ and $y \leq z$.

Thus by sobriety, every irreducible closed set is the closure of a unique point, and hence $\downarrow p = \overline{\{ p \}}$. This means that every directed lower set has a largest element $p$.

Let $A \subseteq X$ be nonempty. BWOC assume $A$ has no maximal element. Choose a strictly increasing sequence $a_0 < a_1 < a_2 < \cdots$ inside $A$. Then $F= \downarrow \{ a_{n}:n \in \mathbb{N} \} = \downarrow p$ for some $p$, meaning $p$ is the largest element of $F$, but $p\leq a_N < a_{N+1} \in F$ for some $N$, which is a contradiction.

Thus every nonempty $A\subseteq X$ has a maximal element $m$, but then $\uparrow m  \cap A = \{ m \}$, so $\{ m \}$ is open and thus isolated in $A$. This implies $X$ is {P51}.
